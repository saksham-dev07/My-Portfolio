# The Curiosity Circuit: world audit and implementation plan

Date: 10 October 2026. Scope: desktop, keyboard and mouse, the existing driving
world and its connection to Saksham's portfolio.

## Direction

Build a personal Indian campus valley where visitors can **play, discover and
understand the work**. The strongest identity is an inhabited place with three
rhythms: an energetic maidan, a green maker/campus precinct, and a quiet garden
below a rocky ridge. Keep the miniature India Gate inspired arch as an identity
accent. Repeat sandstone, terracotta, dark timber, green planting and restrained
copper across functional objects.

Give each addition a purpose: a game, a piece of personal storytelling, a useful
landmark, a place to rest, or a response to curiosity. Compose the existing world
before expanding its footprint. Games remain optional; every project, résumé
and contact action stays directly available through the map.

sites across three rows, all four personal destinations, front/reverse hub

views, and actual first-person camera views along four routes. Twenty-three audit

## Journey and spatial composition

The suggested first visit is 3–5 minutes, with an immediate map shortcut for
visitors who want only the work. These durations are design targets, not measured
session times.

1. **Welcome court, around (0,0).** A small shaded garage/adda, concise controls,
   and a visible fork: Play, Projects, or About. First motion causes one local
   response, such as a hanging pennant lifting or a garage light switching on.
2. **Play maidan, X -59…-23, Y -53…-15.** Bowling and a car skill course share a
   warm court surface. A scoreboard, seating and tree edges define the space.
   Keep the bowling lane at Y -30 and the repaired road entrance at (-28,-42).
3. **Personal hub and Indian garden, around (0,-30) to (2,-56).** The five scenes
   become interactive story moments. A small chai kiosk/bench cluster beside
   the information garden is useful seating and a personal detail, not a new
   unrelated monument. Keep all road and flag/arch clearances.
4. **Maker pavilions, X 46…165, Y -15…-136.** Three landscape chapters, each with
   one identifiable silhouette and a working demonstration; all twelve project
   links remain. Chapter identity changes before visitors reach the turn.
5. **Campus courtyards, X -27…40, Y -71…-124.** Make the story from About to Skills,
   Highlights and Education legible. Plant behind buildings, use low foliage
   beside labels, and maintain clear forecourts and the existing clockwise loop.
6. **Monsoon garden and ridge, around (-50,-115).** A slower ending: lotus pond,
   shaded seating, a lookout and nature notes. The visitor can leave with a
   small locally generated postcard summarizing discoveries and project links.

Break the exposed brown rim into a western rocky ridge, an eastern escarpment
and a few northern hills, with low green saddles between them. Smooth steep
blends before adding traversal: sampled ridge/research slopes currently change
about 1.3–1.45 metres per horizontal metre. Keep the shared visible terrain and
Cannon heightfield generated from the same source.

Use regional vegetation rules instead of index-modulo mixing: mostly green
broadleaf trees in campus/garden, a few terracotta flowering crowns at the maidan,
copper accents in selected research beds, and sparse ridge trees. Existing 68
trees, 182 field props and 42 flower instances are enough to begin with. Reserve
the whole canopy envelope against roads, signs, labels and interaction pads;
use young trees/shrubs in foregrounds and taller groups behind landmarks.

## Five flagship experiences

### 1. Ramp Run: car parkour through the maidan

Reserve a candidate strip inside X -58…-34, Y -49…-36, in the playground's lower
half. Validate it against the retained imported ramps before locking positions;
leave the eastern road entrance and bowling rail approaches clear.

A 30–45 second trial: run-up, shallow take-off ramp, wide landing deck, low
banked turn, then a precision-stop zone. Three ordered checkpoints and a nearby
retry spawn create a complete loop. Show time, checkpoint and result; reward a
clean landing with one brief pennant/particle response. Start with forgiving
platforms wider than the car, low elevation changes and recovery space.

Use existing Cannon raycast wheels; no new jump key. Match ramp mesh slopes
with pitched/convex collision proxies, not a box occupying the empty space below
the visible ramp. Keep decorative edges outside wheel paths. Test suspension,
rollover assistance, side falls, reverse entry, reset and retry before narrowing
the route. Car-compatible parkour is the first traversal feature; it can ship
without a walking controller.

Alongside it, turn existing bowling into **three scored shots**. Pins remain near
(-48,-30), ball near (-28,-30), and the corrected start pad is (-24.5,-30),
behind the ball at the open lane entrance. Its former position (-28.5,-23.5)
overlapped a break wall; clearance now includes actual dynamic brick footprints.
Detect knocked pins by body tilt/displacement after a bounded settling period.
Reuse the ten pins and one ball, clear all motion on reset, and provide a
button/Enter-assisted shot as well as car pushing.

### 2. Campus Rally: a real short driving challenge

Reuse the existing loop: start (2,-98.5), then (40,-112), (2,-124),
(-26.5,-112), and back to start. Add a 3-2-1 countdown, elapsed time, clear next
checkpoint, local best and immediate retry. Store a visual best-run ghost at
20 Hz; it has no physics body and cannot obstruct visitors. Cap sample count.

Extend the existing pure `CircuitProgress` into a session state machine. Count
heading-aware gate crossings in order; reject teleports and reverse shortcuts.
Time the run using active simulation time, pausing with dialogs or lost tab
focus. Starting while already inside the first zone must still work. Update the
HUD at 10 Hz rather than writing to the DOM on every render frame.

### 3. Monsoon Field Notes: nature with responses

Link the rangoli clearing (-58,-60), ridge arch (-49,-80), lookout candidate
near (-60,-100), garden entrance (-50,-108), pond (-54.3,-115) and shelter
(-45,-116). Each offers one brief personal note plus an observation: a lotus
opening with ripples, a bird taking a short flight, a butterfly leading toward
the next viewpoint, or the existing rangoli blooming. Use a handful of pooled
actors, not a constant particle cloud over the whole map.

Discovery is untimed. Observe from a car-accessible spot, click the object, or
use a labelled map action; garden paths do not need to become car roads. Extend
the visited registry to distinguish portfolio stops, activities and discoveries.
Keep sound opt-in and reduced motion immediate. A photo/postcard frame is a
reward that includes links to the work, with no upload or backend required.

### 4. Maker Pavilions: the portfolio becomes playable

Give the three project rows different physical installations, using existing
project content rather than invented claims:

- **Applied systems, around (60,-24):** an AI observation bench. A simple lens
  interaction reveals an annotated sample/explanation. Existing main-portfolio
  forensic demonstrations can supply approved local sample data.
- **Data and operations, around (90,-64):** a tactile route/flow table associated
  with delivery and automation projects. Rearrange a few nodes and see a short
  deterministic response; do not imply this is a live deployed service.
- **Small experiments, around (60,-104):** a playable Ping Pong arcade, connected
  to the Gesture Ping Pong project. Mouse/keyboard works immediately; webcam
  input, if added later, is an explicit optional choice.

The other nine projects remain easy to inspect through smaller supporting
displays. Add readable double-sided chapter signs and a contextual DOM title
near a selected exhibit. Keep the world in one Three renderer; do not import the
main React app and a second Three version into its initial bundle. Share pure
data/state, lazy-load isolated activity modules, and use the established
world-to-portfolio message/navigation seam for full project details.

### 5. Woodland parkour with a walking Saksham avatar

This is a later feature, after actor-aware controls exist. Candidate site:
ridge woodland X -62…-54, Y -96…-84, entrance near (-55,-96). Its steep terrain
needs authored terraces and verified clearances; coordinates are not approved
platform placements yet. A compact course can climb stone steps, cross timber
decks and finish at a lookout. Keep it separate from the driving corridor.

Park the car and explicitly enter walk mode. Use a new rigged low-poly avatar
with the same hair, glasses and clothing as the dioramas; the existing posed
meshes are not locomotion rigs. Initial targets: 4.2 m/s movement, about 1.1 m
jump height, 100 ms coyote time, 120 ms jump buffering and a maximum walkable
slope around 35 degrees. Tune these through playtesting.

WASD moves; Space jumps only in walk mode; Enter interacts; visible controls
return to the parked car or retry the last checkpoint. Preserve left-drag pan
and right-drag rotation in the driving world. A fixed-rotation compound Cannon
body, ground contact checks and fixed-step movement are a plausible first
controller; test stair/edge contact and camera collision before expanding it.
Triggers must use the active actor and elevation, not the car's XY alone.

## Architecture and asset work

```text
World/
  Activities/
    ActivityDirector.js       # active session, pause, retry, input/camera leases
    ActivityRegistry.js       # region, entry, accessible actions, lazy loader
    SessionState.js           # pure ready/countdown/running/result transitions
    Rally/                    # progress, gate crossing, capped ghost recording
    Bowling/                  # settling, scoring, bounded reset
    RampRun/                  # ordered trial checkpoints and recovery spawns
    FieldNotes/               # discoveries and finite pooled nature responses
  Traversal/                  # added only for the walking phase
    ActorAdapter.js           # pose/velocity/bounds for car and walker
    FootController.js
    ActorCamera.js
  Environment/                # deepen existing seams gradually, no mass move
    RegionRules.js            # planting/density/material/sightline exclusions
    GameAssetKit.js            # validated named GLB nodes and collision extras
```

The current Three.js, GSAP and Cannon stack is sufficient. Avoid a second physics
engine or animation framework. Dispose session listeners/effects on exit, pool
dynamic props, cap the legacy exponential Konami spawning, and persist only
versioned local scores/discoveries. No database, account, multiplayer, leaderboard
service or paid Vercel feature is needed.

### First playable pass, 10 October

The shared `ActivityDirector.js` and DOM `ActivityInterface.js` now run scored
Maidan Bowling and Campus Rally. Bowling reuses the existing ten pins and ball,
with three attempts, adjustable assisted aim, car-push play, settle scoring and
clean resets. Rally uses the existing courtyard road with a three-second
countdown, directional ordered gates, a timer, browser-local best time, shortcut
and reset rejection, and retry. Dialogs and hidden tabs pause both games.
Starting another activity stops the previous one; map travel leaves the game.

`World/ActivityProps.js` loads four small props after entry when their districts
come into view. Thrixel supplied a live scoreboard, start arch and low ramp/deck.
The chai cart was subsequently redesigned in Blender with a striped scalloped
canopy, brass tea vessels, kulhad cups and spoked wheels. A custom Blender
trophy and stepped podium also replace the Highlights artwork. All use the
existing world matcap, coarse compound colliders,
and an explicit Y-up to Z-up conversion. The arch has an open car passage; the
ramp collision follows its slope and landing. The chai cart sits beside the
garden path, clear of the pond and planted tree crowns. Scores are drawn by
the runtime on both scoreboard faces.

The Thrixel project uses a shared palette/budget guide. Each draft was inspected
as an exported GLB and in the world, then received a scoped refinement. Static
parts are grouped before shipping; the ramp retains two named surface nodes
for collision validation and is combined into one render mesh on import.
No portrait reference was sent to Thrixel. Raw generation work stays outside
Git and deployment. Games work even when these optional art downloads fail.

This delivers the first games and a few purposeful scene additions. Timed
Ramp Run, nature Field Notes, new project pavilions and walking parkour remain
the next phases; a drivable ramp is not a completed parkour game.

### Additional reliability priorities

Before a larger environment pass, give the core asset loader explicit timeout,
failure and retry states so an unavailable essential file cannot strand the
visitor at loading. Split nonessential legacy props out of the boot bundle.
Maintain direct portfolio/map routes while persisting explored stops locally.
Preserve mouse pan while parked and automatic follow when driving; address
frame-rate-dependent camera easing and preserve a chosen follow zoom. Measure
these independently of terrain/art changes. The terrain clearance, blank
reverse signs and perimeter composition findings above remain open work.

Blender pipeline: separate collections for maidan kit, vegetation variations,
pond life and lookout props; metre scale, grounded pivots, editable parts. Build
silhouettes first, one-segment bevels only where visible, apply transforms, use
shared vertex colours and export named glTF nodes. Keep `.blend` sources outside
deployment and retain reproducible generator scripts in the repository. Validate
winding, normals, bounds, node names and collision extras on the actual exported
GLB. Grass uses instancing and a small regional variation palette.

Initial asset targets, not measured exports: ramp/deck 300–500 triangles, bank
250–400, checkpoint post 80–160, score station 150–250, lotus/reed patch 100–180,
young tree 300–500, stone seating 180–300. Target under 5,000 added triangles and
five added draw calls per small activity, with roughly sixteen or fewer extra
active dynamic bodies. Measure actual whole-frame costs before accepting these
budgets; existing detail-heavy portrait, desk and campus assets need separate
LOD/transfer review. Current production output is about 51.93 MiB; its largest
asset is the 10.63 MiB campus model, and world JS remains about 1.36 MB minified
(382.93 kB gzip). The large existing world chunk remains an optimization task.
Do not add game assets to the boot resource list: load only near a district or
when a visitor starts its activity.

For each phase, repeat overhead, original and first-person checks, plus two
opposite approaches at changed destinations. Test long play sessions, tab blur,
dialogs, repeat retries, offline/slow asset delivery and reduced motion. Target
smooth 60 fps on the agreed desktop test device, with a measured report rather
than an unsupported performance claim. Test CPU/GPU frame time, draw calls,
triangles, body count and memory before/after each phase.

**Recommended first milestone:** reliable controls and a polished short rally
plus scored bowling, followed by one deliberately composed maidan/garden pass.
Then add Ramp Run. This creates a replayable journey quickly and provides the
foundation for real walking parkour.
